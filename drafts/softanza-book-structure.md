# Softanza · The Software Makers Platform of the AI Age
## Rethinking programming, languages and intelligence, from first principles

*The final book structure, settled 2026-10-08 01:25 UTC, after the seven fixes of the assessment. Thirty-four chapters in seven parts, six appendices, about 110,000 words. A spine of nine chapters carries the whole argument and can be read alone.*

Back cover: **Born in Africa. Useful to the World!** · **Declare, then govern.**

Series: **the second of the AYOUNI books**, after *Refinement-Oriented Programming*.

## The shape of every chapter

1. **The concept as the field holds it** — what programmers, tools and the industry mean by it today, in two paragraphs.
2. **The first principle** — the one thing the record returns to, in one sentence.
3. **The Softanza form** — the idea as built, with one running block per claim, its promise under it, run on a named date at a named commit.
4. **The decision, and what was refused** — why it was done this way and what was turned down, from the record's own rulings and memos. *This is the element the site cannot carry, and what makes the book complementary.*
5. **The court** — the guard that proves it, by name and assertion count.
6. **The field, and where it leads** — two or three tools, cited and dated, with the loss beside the win.
7. **The limit** — built, designed or absent, in stage words.
8. **Six doors** — one line each: what the programmer, the analyst, the designer, the decision-maker, the educator and the agent take from the chapter.
9. **The exercise** — one, judged by the tutor's rule, never answered first.

Each chapter links to the site's area and narration for the court's full evidence. The book's code is the argument's minimum; the site's is the court's maximum.

## Part I · The age, the maker, the method

**1. Change arrives faster than trust.** The AI age: code became cheap and trust became scarce. What changed, for whom. The position in one sentence: admission over generation.

**2. The software maker.** The definition: turns what they know about a world into something that runs, without waiting for the software industry; defined by ownership, not by programming skill. The six doors. The agent as a maker. The three makers of this book in a page each: a restaurateur in Lyon, a bank in Niamey, an organisation in the field.

**3. The method, from first principles.** Forty-six concepts rethought, the record's own practice. The two acts the whole platform reduces to: declare a world in a language made for it; govern how it changes. How this book proves what it claims: the chapter shape, the court, the stage words.

**4. The platform in one picture.** The engine, the three layers, the worlds, the courts, the Atlas's anatomy; one figure, ten pages. What is read as a rethink in Parts II to IV is a part of this picture.

## Part II · Programming, rethought

**5. Law: the code adheres to the way a person thinks, and the court judges it whoever wrote it.** What the AI age changes here: machines now write most code, and the style thinned where they did; a discipline must be one a machine can be held to. How chapters 6 to 11 prove the law, and how Part II depends on Part III.

**6. The mental model, and the metaphors.** Select the object, then contains, count, find, apply; walk, check, yield, perform; a condition inside an argument.

**7. The writing style, and the seven goals.** Fourteen rules read from the slides, the narrations and the classic tests; the seven design goals as the architectural half of the same discipline, held across thirty-three features by one architect.

**8. Functions as linguistic expressions, and scope.** The twenty-two forms a name can take; scope-oriented programming, the invisible frame named and chosen in the verb.

**9. Patterns of thought: the Softanzuter, Reaxis and the engines.** Regex as a computational medium; the ladder from pattern to thinking machine; reactive programming in plain words; truth, quantifiers, time.

**10. Programming becomes refinement.** The refinement point, the four-stage gate, the cascade, reversibility; the practice renamed, and the library verb beneath the product.

## Part III · Languages, rethought

**11. Law: a language is declared and closed, and whatever speaks it, person or model, is judged by it.** What the AI age changes here: the writer of a sentence may be a model. Dependency: Part II's discipline lives inside declared languages.

**12. A language, declared.** The factory; closed grammars with no host escape; the court born with the grammar; the grammar emitted so a model can only speak valid sentences; the facets a language may wear.

**13. Natural, and executable: two directions, one discipline.** A sentence runs because every word is in a dictionary the library owns, and what it does not know it refuses with the reason; near-natural chains where the human is the parser; four languages as data; the history, from the 2020 slide to the three eras.

**14. Knowledge programming.** One file that is a system: entities, relations, an ontology, rules; adding a fact augments the intelligence with no code and no training.

**15. Pattern languages.** Regex elevated to lists, numbers, tables, matrices, time and graphs; a pattern declared, not written.

**16. Seven languages, one door.** The rationale per language, the external door, and the difference between a facet and a door.

## Part IV · Intelligence, rethought

**17. Law: programmatic by default, the model last, admission over generation.** What the AI age changes here, which is everything in this part. The stack without a model: perceive, decide, react, learn. Dependency: governed by Parts II and III.

**18. The five registers, and conversations that ask.** An option, a data structure, a formula, a sentence, an example; the gap generates the question; the answer admitted, enumerated or refused with reasons.

**19. The domain language model.** The next thing a model says is a checkable unit of the world, never a token; rung one without neurons, rung two teacher-free; the grammar as the gate.

**20. Agents that cannot hurt you.** The agent proposes, the workbench rehearses, the court judges, only a governed actor commits; the mind programmatic by default.

**21. The court.** Promises as the house's test form; narrations that cannot lie; what no counter can see; security and performance as courts, with the ledger, the sentinel, the honest stopwatch and the governed loop.

**22. Compared, honestly.** The field, head to head, where it leads, losses published with wins; the method of the comparison itself.

## Part V · The platform, assembled

**23. A world, a constellation, a platform, and the architect.** stzApp, stzSuperApp, stzPlatform; the three scopes; the platform architect as a role.

**24. Rehearse, then commit.** The twin, emulation, deployment; code first, subscribe later; production refuses a fake.

**25. Your users keep their medium.** Conversations as a projection: the messaging app, the text menu, the voice; a medium adds renderings, never verbs.

**26. The environment.** The IDE pattern: one shell, two projections, a facet ribbon by lifecycle, a rail that proposes, a status bar that tells the truth; the twenty-two verbs.

**27. The engine beneath.** Three layers and one dependency rule; the Zig engine; Haro, the surface kept and the runtime measured, with its loss published.

## Part VI · Software makers, forged

**29. A bank in Niamey.** An organisation judged by its own chart, under a regulator's rules.

**30. An organisation in the field.** Rules as data a person changes; the agent that prepares and never decides; offline named as a gap.

**31. What the projects did not prove.** The platforms run on ordinary stacks today; the declarations being written as Softanza's own.

## Part VII · What is owed
