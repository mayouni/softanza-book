The interface constitution lives in its own repository, and the site says that repository is not public yet. This book calls it the interface constitution and does not name the product it belongs to. The library holds only the slot for its verbs and the finder of the elements that serve none; the list itself is the constitution's. So this appendix prints what I read from the constitution's own file on 9 October 2026: its version is 3.11, last updated on 16 August 2026, with seven articles, thirty-one sets and one hundred and twenty-two rules. The counts in this sentence are the file's own, and I counted none of them myself.

Chapter 26 shows how the library finds an element that serves no verb. Chapter 25 shows what a new medium adds. Chapter 20 shows the right to a human as the end of the loop.

### The four axioms

Its four axioms are calm over power, intent over mechanics, visible over hidden, business over technology. Its preamble says business software is not entertainment, and that its interface should not impress, and should serve.

### The seven articles

| Article | Title | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| I | Calm | the interface must never compete with the operator's thinking; calm is a functional requirement and not an aesthetic choice |
| II | Intent Over Mechanics | interaction starts from what the human is trying to do, and widgets are consequences of intent, never primitives; selection never implies execution |
| III | Visible State | if the system knows something important, the user must see it: active filters, pending drafts, norms, background work, machine provenance |
| IV | Reversibility | every act is reversible, or explicitly and visibly irreversible: staged, named and reviewed |
| V | Predictable Authority | every consequential act names its actor, and an intelligent agent is an actor under this law, never an authority above it |
| VI | Operator Primacy and Vibes | business users are operators, not explorers; vibes, densities, dialects and surfaces are equivalent vibes of one truth |
| VII | Amendment | the law grows only from evidence; removing a protection requires proof the harm is gone |

### The twenty-two verbs

The grammar is closed: rules quantify over these verbs, and a new medium adds renderings, never verbs. Six families, each with its invariant, as the constitution writes them.

| Family | Verbs | Invariant |
|---|---|---|
| Orienting | discover, understand, locate | discovery must be satisfiable at every entry point |
| Attention | focus, filter, compare | an active filter is visible state; focus and discovery cannot both be at maximum |
| Information | read, scan, scroll, zoom | a read surface that silently acts is an intent violation |
| Selection | select, highlight, preview, mark | selection expresses interest, never commitment |
| Action | act, confirm, cancel, undo | every act has a reachable undo, or declares why it cannot |
| Continuity | pause, resume, retry, exit | pause, resume and exit are always reachable; a trapped flow is unconstitutional |

That makes three, three, four, four, four and four: twenty-two. Of them, the library's worlds (chapter 26) know five as screen intents: discover, understand, focus, select and act. The library does not check the word.

### The six rights

| Right | What it says |
|---|---|
| to understand | every element, denial and setting can explain itself in the operator's language |
| to reverse | what was done can be undone, and what cannot says so before, not after |
| to know | the state of the system, filters, drafts, progress and provenance, is always visible |
| to leave | every flow, focus and overlay has a reachable exit that returns the user to where they stood |
| to act without fear | no element creates anxiety through unpredictability, irreversibility by default or hidden consequences |
| to a human | wherever a machine serves, a person is reachable, clearly and without penalty |

The last right is enforced by a single rule, and that rule is marked as a matter of judgment: a person decides whether it is met, and no machine checks it. The library does not mention it.

### Intelligence under law

Set 31 is the constitution's answer to the agent, and it is where chapter 20 meets it. Its ten rules, with how each is enforced, as the file records it:

| Rule | Title | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| 97 | The Agent Is an Actor | judgment |
| 98 | Proposals, Not Commits | judgment |
| 99 | Visible Provenance | a machine, later |
| 100 | Deterministic Islands | a machine, later |
| 101 | The Dignity Contract | judgment |
| 102 | The Right to a Human | judgment |
| 103 | Grounded Proactivity | judgment |
| 104 | Machine Legibility | a machine, later |
| 121 | The Assistant Never Masks the Work | judgment |
| 124 | The Generator Ships a Working Thing | judgment |

Rule 98 is the law of chapter 20's plan, written for an interface: a proposal is not a commit. Rule 99 asks for the mark on machine-made content; chapter 14's provenance is the nearest thing the library builds. Rule 104 asks for machine legibility; chapter 26's accessibility tree is the nearest thing the library builds: what a screen reader can operate, an agent can operate. Seven of the ten are enforced by judgment, and three by a machine that is to come.

### What the constitution forbids

The constitution lists seventeen forbidden patterns, each tied to the rules it violates. Among them are modal storms, invisible active filters, hidden destructive actions, and selection that silently executes. Its retired numbers are never reused, and two numbers are burned and reserved for ever.

### What this book takes from it, and what it does not

It takes the closed grammar, which it uses in chapters 25 and 26; the six rights, of which it uses one; and the sets, of which it uses the one for agents, and Rule 118 on the five values of status. It does not take the rest. No guard of the library judges a shell against the verbs, and the library's own design text still lists twenty-four verbs from an interaction engine that I removed on 26 May 2026. The constitution's list is twenty-two.
