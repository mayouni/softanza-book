# 2026-10-14 · The book read on the author's behalf, and what the reading changed

The author, 14 October: "Read the book on my behalf, after getting step back from your role as a writer to embrace the profile of a reader." Eight fresh readers, who had not written a line of it and read nothing about how it was made, read the English book as readers under `research/READER-BRIEF.md`: whole pages in order, then again with the questions in hand; a verdict per page, where "ready" means they would put the author's name on it. Reports: `research/read/A.md` to `H.md`. Then eight fixers applied the reports under `research/READ-FIX-BRIEF.md`, which records the decisions taken for the whole book on the author's delegation. The build re-ran every block afterwards: 283 of 283 kept, the four changed fences included. `check_fr --all` ok (three pre-existing warnings), audit clean, lint: forbidden 0 in both languages.

`status: read` was not set on any page: the banner says the author has not read it, and that stays true until he does. This memo gives him the page list and what each reading found, so that his own reading can be short.

## What the fixers changed (both languages, same sentences)

- **The voice.** First person everywhere ("the author", "his", "a researcher ran" are gone). No making-of on the page: notes to self, "I flag it for the author", "I have corrected it there", editor's parentheses, raw file names, pointers to memos and to numbered sections of the study. "The record" and "the study" are defined once, in the preface's "What to trust", and used plainly after. The stake ("I hold this as a view, not a result") stands once per chapter.
- **The former host language.** The library's own names inside code stay (RING-01; the preface now says so in one sentence). The book's own example data no longer spells the old name: chapter 5's `"RIxxNxG"` is `"HAxxRxO"`, chapter 8's letters spell `HARO`; the build judged the predicted outputs and they held. Chapter 16's block calls `python` by name instead of printing a path of the author's disk.
- **One word: facet**, for a second syntax of Haro (chapter 12 defines it; 11 and 16 use it).
- **Two pages that disagreed, settled by the run or the record:** chapter 7 ran named parameters in two orders and chapter 8 swapped positional arguments (each now says which); chapter 1's demo account is what the checklist of the record holds, chapter 2 states the same fact without correcting it, chapter 25 states it without a flag, chapter 29 quotes what chapter 1 now says; chapter 33's 3,461 and chapter 27's 3,425 are the same day's two memo entries, and chapter 33 says so; chapter 23's commons is what the run printed; chapter 26's "by the guard's account" is "read by hand, 8 October".
- **Chapter 1** (author-owned, on the delegation and only these): the mail scene has its real registry block, made from chapter 2's guard, and the prose reads what it prints; the preface promise it cites is now made by the preface; seven languages (the host and six runtimes); the demo sentence narrowed to the record. Its front matter `edits` records this.
- **Numbers and names a reader can check:** numbered laws cited to Appendix C where it lists them, dropped where it does not; `UpdateWith` reaches `Update()`; one floor's three names tied in one sentence (`stzPlatform`, `stzPlatformProfile`, `stzDelivery`, all in the snapshot); chapter 27's prefix counts explained against 714; chapter 31's refused figure (a progress share) told apart from its measured one (chapter 27's 1.04 to 1.88).
- **Shape:** chapter 33 ends once (the exercise moved above the closing line); chapter 18's second exercise is no longer answered by section 3; the duplicated viz block kept in chapter 8 only; the glossary gains estate, house rules, Principal, record, session, stamp, study, and spells artefact; the map carries its seven part headings and an Appendices heading; Appendix F counts the narrations from the folder instead of typing "about a hundred and thirty-five"; Appendix G's table lists the appendices too.
