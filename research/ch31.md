## 2. The concept as the field holds it

The field has a habit for saying what a piece of work has proved, and it has three forms. The first is the **reference**: a
logo on a home page, a customer story of two pages, the sentence "trusted by". It proves that someone paid or signed; it does
not say what they ran, for how long, or what they said. The second is the **readiness scale**: a level that names how far a
technology has travelled from a laboratory to the place it was built for (the highest level is a system proven in operation).
It is a vocabulary for stage, which the record shares in its own words (built, designed, absent). The third is the
**limits section**: in empirical software engineering a study must state its threats to validity, and a machine-learning model
ships with a card whose last part is caveats. It is the form closest to this chapter, and the one most often skipped when the
reader is a buyer.

What the three have in common is that the claim and its proof travel apart. A page says "used by" and the proof is a trust
in the page. The field's tools are honest where there is a body to enforce it (a paper is reviewed; an artifact is
evaluated and badged); they are silent where there is only a seller and a buyer.

Named practices, each marked as it is held:
- **Technology readiness levels** (NASA, later ISO 16290:2013; general knowledge). Chapter 33 already uses them; do not
  re-tell. One line only: the scale asks "where was it proven", not "who saw it work and what did they say".
- **Threats to validity** in empirical software engineering (Wohlin, Runeson, Höst, Ohlsson, Regnell, Wesslén,
  *Experimentation in Software Engineering*, Springer, 2012: internal, external, construct and conclusion validity; general
  knowledge). The author of a study lists what the study cannot support. The record's page "What the projects did not prove"
  is the same act for a product.
- **Model cards** (Mitchell and others, "Model Cards for Model Reporting", FAT* 2019; general knowledge): a model ships with
  its intended use and its caveats. The record's stage word beside every product (`estate.md`) is a card in one word.
- **Artifact evaluation badges** (ACM "Artifact Review and Badging", versions 2016 and 2020; general knowledge): available,
  evaluated, reproduced. A claim is badged by what someone else did with it. The book's proof line ("k of n blocks kept")
  is that badge for a chapter.

The record's own field study of the practice, by file and date:
- `customers.md` (2026-10-04), the rule: "Nothing is a reference until a document proves it." It also prints no number of
  countries and no number of developers.
- `forged.md` (2026-10-04), "How to read this page": a lesson is listed only when a file carries it, a project only as far
  as a document attests it, and where the library only uses a project as its example, the page says "example", not "origin".
- Study 12.22 (2026-10-07 22:50 UTC), the comparative tone: every claim about another tool "cited, dated and run", and every
  loss "published with the wins" (heading of 12.22, line 895).
- What the field leads in, and the site says so itself: production (section 7).
