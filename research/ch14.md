## 2. The concept as the field holds it

Three practices, in the order a reader of the field meets them. The record's own field study is thin on this subject
(it compares graph libraries, not knowledge systems), so most of this section is **general knowledge**, marked, with the
dates to be checked before print.

1. **The semantic stack: RDF, OWL, SPARQL.** A fact is a triple of subject, predicate and object, written in a shared
   vocabulary; an ontology states what classes and properties exist and what follows from them (a reasoner derives the
   consequences); a query language asks the triples. These are W3C Recommendations (RDF 1.1 in 2014, OWL 2 in 2009,
   SPARQL 1.1 in 2013). General knowledge, not a study of the record. What the field asks of the author of a knowledge
   base is an ontology language of its own, a store and a query language, usually beside the application, not in it.
2. **Property-graph databases: Neo4j and its query language Cypher.** The record has one comparison that names it:
   `base/doc/narrations/stzgraph-vs-other-graph-libraries.md` ("Softanza stzGraph vs. Modern Graph Ecosystem", line 1),
   which says on its second line "By QwenAI" (so it is a model's text kept in the library, not the author's). Its last
   commit in the library's live checkout is dated 2025-12-26 (`git log` on `D:\GitHub\stzlib`; the snapshot has no history,
   so the date is read from the live checkout, for this one date only). It credits the field with scale, persistence and
   transactions ("Enterprise scale", "ACID compliant", line 150 to 160 of that file). It describes the graph databases as
   systems that "prioritize persistent storage and query efficiency at scale" (paraphrase of line 14).
3. **Rules over facts: Prolog and Datalog.** Facts and rules in one program, a query by resolution. General knowledge
   (Prolog from the 1970s, Datalog from the 1980s). The record knows it as a language to reach, not to rebuild: the
   intelligence architecture lists "Prolog for logic" among the languages the external-code door runs
   (`SOFTANZA_INTELLIGENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md`, lines 169 to 171), and the study's 12.28 names a Prolog class among the
   external doors. A fourth practice stands behind the chapter's contrast and is named in the record: retrieval, a pile of
   document fragments searched by similarity (the design document says "No RAG machinery", line 42).

The shape of the contrast, for the chapter: in the field the knowledge lives in a store and its logic in a reasoner or a
rule engine, both beside the program; in Softanza the knowledge is a plain text file the program loads, with its laws in
the same file.
