## 2. The concept as the field holds it

Three readings of "who makes software" are current, and the chapter must place the record's word against them.

1. **The maker movement** (general knowledge): the hobbyist culture of workshops, 3D printers and electronics
   associated with *Make:* magazine (Dale Dougherty, 2005) and Maker Faires. The record names the risk itself: 
   "'Makers' can read, in some markets, as the hobbyist movement of workshops and printers; the site's definition
   disarms it and the book's first page must repeat that definition before anything else" (study 12.30, amendment
   of 2026-10-08 01:02). The author's cover ruling of 01:12 chose "Software Makers" partly for this: "'Software
   Makers' closes the hobbyist reading this proposal had flagged" (study 12.30, 01:12).
2. **Low-code and the "citizen developer"** (general knowledge for the terms, coined in industry-analyst usage in the
   2010s): business people building applications on a vendor's visual platform. **The record holds a dated, first-hand
   instance**: the organisation in Niger's own specification "privilégie une plateforme construite sur Microsoft 365
   en « low-code », c'est-à-dire avec peu de programmation" (`diko/output/etude/_source/chapters/01-cadre.md:24`, study
   v1.2 of 2026-10-08). That is the field's answer, written by a client, before the author's offer changed it (see 5).
3. **The coding agent and "vibe coding"** (general knowledge for the phrase, Andrej Karpathy, February 2025; the record's
   own field study for the tools): the site's page *For coding agents* names "Claude Code, Codex or Cursor" as agents
   that "read files, search them, edit them and run commands" and says what they lack: to "know an unfamiliar API
   without guessing, judge their work against rules and promises, and present a change as an intention a person can
   review" (`stzsite/content/en/coding-agents.md:11`, site HEAD 2026-10-08). The site's *Wise coding* page defines vibe
   coding as "prompting a machine and keeping whatever comes out" (`content/en/wise.md:5`).

What the three share: the maker is defined by a **tool** (a workshop, a vendor platform, a model), and the artefact
lives where the tool lives. The record's definition moves the weight to **ownership** of the artefact.

## 3. The first principle

The one sentence, as the site prints it (`stzsite/content/en/offering.md:11`):

> "A maker turns what they know about a world into something that runs, without waiting for the software industry.
> They are not defined by programming skill but by ownership: the artefact is theirs, in plain text, and it does not
> expire."

French twin (`content/fr/offering.md:11`): « Un maker transforme ce qu'il sait d'un monde en quelque chose qui tourne,
sans attendre l'industrie du logiciel. Il n'est pas défini par sa maîtrise de la programmation mais par la propriété :
l'artefact est à lui, en texte brut, et il n'expire pas. »

The home page carries the "software maker" variant, EN and FR (`stzsite/index.html:65-66` and `:57-58`): "Someone who
turns what they know about a world into software that runs, without waiting for the software industry: a teacher who
declares a course, an analyst who declares a bank's rules, a merchant who declares a shop, a student who declares a
game."

The sentence the definition rests on, older and ratified by the author: "Not 'use our language' — declare *yours*:
the language of your domain, in your jargon" (`softanza/vision/01-KERNEL.md:12-13`, "Ratified in session by the
author"). The 2022 rationale says the same for people: programming "accessible to all ... with just one obligation:
the ability of thinking logically about the problem in hand" (deck slide 2, as the study quotes it, 12.2).

**Provenance warning.** The definition's wording is a session's: drafted 2026-10-01 in
`stzsite/doc/LEARNING-FROM-WOLFRAM.md` §4, "draft for the author's ruling"; the memo of that night lists
"STZSITE-DEFINITIONS-01: the author rules the three definitions" as waiting (`softanza/memos/2026-10-01.md:154-157`);
no ruling of it is filed in `memos/`. The home's "software maker" variant is "my wording, the author's to change"
(`memos/2026-10-08.md:121`). The author's book structure (2026-10-08 01:25) adopts its substance ("defined by
ownership, not by programming skill"). See question 13.3.

## 5. The decision, and what was refused

From rulings, charters and memos only. "Author" marks the author's own ruling; "session" marks a session's words.

   "The first audience stays the programmer in a small team, in red. The agentic era did not replace that person; it
   multiplied them" (`softanza/vision/08-NORTH-STAR.md` §5, lines 408-410; the chapter is "ratified 2026-09-27 in session

   get:** the capability to commit" (`offering.md:96`). The rule in the library's words: "an LLM proposes, only a
   pi-gate commits" (blocks 6, 8). *Refused:* an agent that decides.
4. **"Software Makers", not "Makers".** Author, 2026-10-08 01:12: the cover "The Software Makers Platform of the AI
   Age"; the session's gloss on it: "'Software Makers' closes the hobbyist reading" (study 12.30, 01:12). *Refused:*

7. **A reference needs a document.** The site's rule, its own words: "Nothing is a reference until a document proves

## 9. Six doors

As the record supports them (Offering, `offering.md:15-106`; the 2025 stack slide's five audiences, study 12.2).

- **The programmer.** You declare your intent in a library that reads like a sentence; you get a Zig engine under
  every call (Door 1). The North Star keeps you first (decision 1).
- **The analyst.** You declare "your organisation's entities, rules and flows, in a plain-text knowledge file"; you
  get a queryable world (Door 2). A rule is data you attach, and the model judges itself (block 8).
- **The designer.** You declare "a picture, a map, a screen, as a program"; you get a render "reproducible to the
  byte" and a constitution a machine can check (Door 3). The restaurant's two-gesture rule is a designer's rule born of
  a real user (`REGLES-UX.md`).
- **The decision-maker.** You declare "your world, your governance, and what each actor, human or agent, may commit";
  you get "ownership of everything: code, configuration, data, in plain text" (Door 4), and "Nothing anyone else can
  withdraw" (`sovereignty.md:4`).
- **The educator.** You declare "a course, an overlay for your institution, an edition in your language"; you get a
  program where every exercise is checked by running, and a tutor that asks (Door 5), with its limits printed beside it.
- **The agent.** You declare yourself in an agent file, "what you cover, the reversibility of your acts"; you get a
  workbench and a court; "**What you do not get:** the capability to commit" (Door 6).
