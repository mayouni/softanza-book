## 2. The concept as the field holds it

**What the field means by "intelligence" in software today.** An intelligent application is one with a large
language model inside it. The industry's "agent" is, in the record's own summary, "an LLM in a loop with tools,
memory, and a goal" (`SOFTANZA_INTELLIGENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §5.6, 2026-07-13). The agents narration lists the
2026 builder's "shopping list": "a Python project, a model provider, a graph framework, a vector store, a set of
tools with typed schemas, a tracing service, and a chapter of safety advice" (`stz-agents-that-cannot-hurt-you-narration.md`,
opening; it says it paraphrases "a 2026 agentic-AI engineering roadmap", undated beyond the year). Safety, in that
picture, is "a property of the agent: alignment, guardrails, confirmation prompts, 'human in the loop' checkboxes"
(`Softaza and Agents That Cannot Hurt You.md`, opening; the document carries no date in its header).

Named tools and practices, with sources:
- **The LLM agent loop, framework style.** The design document names "LangChain-style tool use, AutoGPT
  descendants, computer-use agents in sandboxed VMs" and judges that they "all place the boundary at the wrong
  layer" (`Softaza and Agents That Cannot Hurt You.md`, "Where the field stands"). The site's Atlas line calls the
  agents group "a governed inversion of LangChain" (study 12.18, "Where it is told today"). The ReAct pattern
  (reason-and-act prompting, Yao et al., 2022) is the loop's common name: general knowledge.
- **Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).** Lewis et al., 2020: general knowledge. The record refuses it by name:
  "No code change. No training. No RAG machinery." (§0.1) and "derivation replaces RAG" (§5.6).
- **Vibe coding.** The word is the field's (general knowledge: coined by Andrej Karpathy, February 2025); the
  record defines it on its own terms: "the human prompts and the machine guesses" (§0.3; the site's `wise.md`
  lede: "the practice of prompting a machine and keeping whatever comes out").
- **Structured output by grammar-constrained decoding** (GBNF grammars as in llama.cpp, 2023: general knowledge).
  The record's own form is C9 v1.0.0, `base/neural/SOFTANZA_STRUCTURED_OUTPUT.md`, first published 2026-08-22,
  "extracted from working code, not designed ahead of it".

## 9. Six doors

- **The programmer.** Your agent's mind is code you can test with ordinary assertions; a model may be plugged in
  as one faculty and can never hold an effect (`agentgraph_narrated`; narration's table, "Testing").
- **The analyst.** A rule or a fact you add augments the system's intelligence with "No code change. No training."
  (§0.1), and every answer carries its Why (Block 1, Block 4).
- **The designer.** The system asks the user, one accountable question at a time, as a frame any door can render:
  "CLI, agent, web" (`stzConversation.ring` header; Block 2).
- **The decision-maker.** A decision costs "Zero, offline, deterministic" and the domain model ships "FREE" to its
  users (narration's table; §5.9, the DLM ruling); the four reserved agents are the honest gap.
- **The educator.** The conversation asks and never answers first; neural fluency is used "only when a model is
  really there" (`wisecoding_rich_narrated` header), so a learner always sees the floor.
- **The agent.** You may propose; you may not commit; a refusal names its law ("lacks PERMISSION (can)", Block 8),
  and §0.3 / LAW 6 say "An agent should be able to learn Softanza FROM Softanza and be corrected BY Softanza."
