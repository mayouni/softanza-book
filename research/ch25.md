## 2. The concept as the field holds it

"Conversational commerce", or "omnichannel": a business reaches its customers in the channels they already use, and a dialogue
engine sits behind them. Practices (general knowledge, not a study of the record; every date and every provider rule to be
re-read before print, as study 12.25 says in its own "Not measured"):

- **Dialogue frameworks (Rasa, Dialogflow and kin).** They own the dialogue model; the designer writes intents and flows in the
  framework's notation; the framework understands free text with trained models and ships connectors to channels. Study 12.25
  says the same ("The field, in the record's manner").
- **The business messaging platform of a messaging app (WhatsApp Business is the one the study names).** Messages arrive by webhook;
  the business may answer freely inside a service window the customer opened, and outside it only with approved templates, through
  a verified business account. Study 12.25: "sends freely inside a service window opened by the customer, and outside it only
  approved templates". The exact length of the window (the study says twenty-four hours) is general knowledge: re-read the provider.
- **The operator gateways: SMS and USSD.** SMS is billed per segment (the record measures this itself, block S-1). USSD is a
  session of short screens with a timeout, through an operator's gateway, country by country, on any phone without data (study 12.25).
  No file of the snapshot says anything about USSD (grep: zero hits).
- **Platform speech.** The record's own: "Windows has SAPI; macOS has AVSpeechSynthesizer and `say`; every modern browser has
  `speechSynthesis`" (`sound/SOFTANZA_VOICE_PLAN.md` §0, 2026-08-13).

What they share: the dialogue is written once per channel or once per framework, and the channel is a connector, a deployment
detail. That is the picture the chapter turns around: the world is declared once, and the medium is a rendering of it.

## 4. The Softanza form: verified blocks

The thesis as a shape: the library ships the question as **data** (force, subject, relation, options, reason), the **verbs** (ask,
reply, refuse, conclude) and the **court**. A medium is a **rendering** of the data and a **reader** of the reply, both written
by the crafter in a few lines. Nothing in the library renders a question to a phone. What it ships is an English line, `AskIn`,
which is already a text menu: "(1) cheese  (2) olives". Say that, in those words.

Two helper functions are used by the blocks that follow. In the book they sit at the foot of the first block that uses them (the
runner moves a column-0 `func` to the tail of the chapter's file; define each once per chapter):

```haro
func ToText(aQ)
	cOut = "Which " + aQ[:relation] + " for " + aQ[:subject] + "? (" + aQ[:why] + ")"
	for i = 1 to len(aQ[:options])
		cOut += " " + i + ") " + aQ[:options][i]
	next
	return cOut + ". Reply with the number."

func ReadReply(cText)
	for c in cText
		if NOT isdigit(c)
			return cText
		ok
	next
	return 0 + cText
```

**S-3 (kept). The reader of the medium is the medium's job: a text "2" is a value, not a choice.**

## 9. Six doors

- **The programmer.** The question comes back as data (`AskInXT`: force, subject, relation, options, why). You write the rendering and the reader for your medium in ten lines (`ToText`, `ReadReply`), and every reply goes through the same door whatever carried it.
- **The analyst.** The goal is the schema of the conversation; the transcript says which message produced which fact; the sandbox counts the bill (four messages, four segments in S-2) before a provider exists.
- **The designer.** Your user keeps the phone they have: a text line with numbers, or a spoken phrase. The interface constitution's verbs are the grammar you render, and a verb the medium cannot render should be a red cell, not a silent omission (designed, not built: the matrix does not exist).
- **The decision-maker.** A conversation by SMS can be priced in a test: two Arabic dish names moved a 93-character question from one segment to two. The question to ask a vendor, what refuses a sentence your business did not approve, has an answer here (S-10, a closed set).
- **The educator.** The tutor already says the missing step as a question in four languages, in templates that carry no code. A learner on a plain phone is a case the record names and does not yet serve (no USSD, no messaging app).
- **The agent.** You may propose a reply; only a sentence of the closed set passes (S-10), an invented one is refused with its reason, and you cannot give a meaning to a phrase: the author declares it (S-7).
