## 2. The concept as the field holds it

Two readings, and the chapter sets the organisation's story between them.

1. **Low-code on a vendor's suite.** *General knowledge:* the category was named "low-code" by an industry-analyst firm
   in 2014 and grew through the 2010s around the large vendors' office and cloud suites, which sell visual building of
   forms, flows and data on their own platform, usually licensed by seat or by usage; the person who builds is called a
   "citizen developer". The record holds no field study of low-code (searched `stzsite/content/en`, `stzsite/doc`,
   `softanza/vision`, the library's `doc/`, the memos: nothing). **What the record holds is a dated, first-hand instance:**
   the organisation's own specification (June 2026, number NY-168-2026 in the study) "privilégie une plateforme construite
   sur Microsoft 365 en « low-code », c'est-à-dire avec peu de programmation"
   (`diko/output/etude/_source/chapters/01-cadre.md:24`, study v1.2 of 2026-10-08). The book describes the product
   ("a large vendor's office suite"), as chapter 2 does. The same specification already asked for what a seat-licensed
   platform answers poorly: "la réversibilité totale: export des données et de la configuration, propriété acquise à
   DIKO, aucun verrou propriétaire" (ENF10, `01-cadre.md:20`), maintenance "par une équipe réduite" (ENF9), and offline
   work (ENF3). *The writer may point out that the specification's own list of non-functional demands is where the offer
   found its ground; that is my reading of `01-cadre.md:20`, not a sentence of the record.*
2. **Offline-first and the human in the loop.** *General knowledge, well established:* "local-first software" (Kleppmann
   and others, 2019) and the offline-first practice keep the device's copy primary and synchronise later, often with
   conflict-free replicated data types (Shapiro and others, 2011); agent frameworks add an approval step before an agent
   acts. The record's own field note on this is the library's design plan, which refuses the heavy end: "No quorum, no
   leader election, no CRDTs" (`base/cluster/SOFTANZA_DISTRIBUTION_PLAN.md:94-95`, plan of 2026-08-07, in the "kill
   list"). The study's own conflict rule is the simple one: "la première saisie l'emporte, la seconde est refusée avec
   son motif" (`04-architecture.md:107`).

What both readings share: the tool decides who may change the rules (a vendor's builder, a vendor's licence, a vendor's
approval flow). The record's answer moves the weight to **who owns the rules and the vocabulary**.

### Block 5 · authority is not permission: the assistant may prepare, a person's authority must cover the risk: KEPT
Claim: an actor may *hold the permission* for an act and still be refused, because its authority level (advisory,
delegated, autonomous, emergency override) does not cover the act's risk tier; the verdict says why. This is the
mechanical form of "l'agent IA propose, une personne décide" (`03-principes.md:31`) and of "la plateforme vérifie ensuite
les droits, les règles et la possibilité de revenir en arrière" (`04-architecture.md:149`). Source: guard
`governance_narrated`, scene 1 ("CAN is not SHOULD", `base/test/governance/governance_narrated.ring:13-38`); the code is
`governance/stzGovernance.ring:235-257` (`MayProceed`). Chapter 17 used the *permission* refusal ("lacks PERMISSION") of
the pi agent; this is the other refusal, *authority*, which chapter 17 did not show. 11 lines. **Say in prose** that the
library knows an actor only by a name and an authority level; "person" versus "agent" is what the level is declared to be
(see 8).

```haro
oGov = new stzGovernance("hub")

oGov.DeclareRisk("release-advance", 3)
oGov.GrantPermission("assistant", "prepare-file")
oGov.GrantPermission("assistant", "release-advance")
```

## 9. Six doors

As the record supports them.

  edited rule still looks at anything. The local developer's first exam is the same: "modifier une règle et voir tout ce
  qu'elle entraîne" (`10-feuille-de-route.md:62`).

  rule looked at (blocks 1-3); the study's analyst-facing promise is rules "lisibles par la Direction comme par le
  développeur local" (`04-architecture.md:42`).
- **The designer.** The study's screen rules: one text size, a verb on each button, an error that says how to repair, an
  action that can be undone rather than a confirmation (`03-principes.md`, last section). The interface constitution
  (described, not named) is the court for them; this chapter holds no block for it.
- **The decision-maker.** You keep ownership: the rules, the vocabulary, the data, the code, a hand-over to a developer of
  your own country, no licence per seat (offer, `01-cadre.md:30`); and you keep the last word, as a level of authority
  that must cover the risk (block 5) and a decision that is recorded and never erased (block 6).
- **The educator.** The local developer is taught the rules, the vocabulary and the engine over twelve weeks "de travail en
  commun", with the course in four languages (`04-architecture.md:204`). The record is silent on whether that course

- **The agent.** You prepare, check the documents, relance, draft; you do not validate, disburse, publish, order or write
  into the accounts (the study's table); a refusal of what you propose reaches a person with the context (block 4);
  your authority is advisory and the library says so when you try to go further (block 5).
