## 2. The concept as the field holds it

What the field means by "proven in use": a platform is trusted when someone else runs it for real. Three named practices:

1. **Customer logos and case studies.** Vendors print "used in production by…" on their home page. *General knowledge.*
   The record's own answer is the opposite rule, printed on the site: "Nothing is a reference until a document proves it.
   Two are, today." (`stzsite/content/en/customers.md`, lede). And: "This site quotes no number of countries and no number
   of developers." (same file).
2. **Dogfooding and self-hosting.** A team uses its own product for its own work; a compiler compiles itself. The term
   "eating our own dog food" spread from Microsoft in the late 1980s. *General knowledge; date not read from the record.*
   The record's nearest instance: the engine run under the estate's own VM, "What was proved against a demo bridge
   yesterday is proved on the engine itself today" (`softanza/memos/2026-09-11.md`, ringpp memo, line 319).
3. **Technology readiness levels.** NASA's scale, standardised as ISO 16290:2013: the top level is a system proven in its
   operational environment. *General knowledge.* The record's own stage words (built, in construction, specification,
   ratified proposal, named) are its readiness scale (`stzsite/content/en/estate.md`, "Every product, with its stage").
4. **Plan, then apply.** The record cites the field for its rehearsal model: "CI job DAGs with `needs`, Terraform's
   resource graph, apply-or-revert" and "Kubernetes `resources.requests` + the scheduler"
   (`base/doc/design/SOFTANZA_DEPLOYMENT_PLAN.md` §1, file created 2026-07-22). And the field's simulators: "simulators
   *lie*. They mock the database, stub the device, approximate the compute" (`SOFTANZA_EMULATION.md` §1, 2026-07-22).

Where the field puts the proof: in production traffic and logos. Where the record puts it: in a document, a named
perceiver, and a quoted verdict.
