## 2. The concept as the field holds it

**Paragraph one, what a "language" means today.** For the working programmer a language is a general-purpose host
(Python, JavaScript, Java) and a "domain language" is usually an internal DSL: a library whose calls read like
sentences but whose files may contain any host code. The estate's own field study names three such families and the
reason each failed: internal DSLs, "Gradle, Chef, RSpec — died of openness": "A Gradle build file is 'declarative'
until someone writes arbitrary Groovy in it" (`D:\GitHub\ringua\docs\landscape.md`, §1.3, "founding analysis,
2026-08-06"). Language workbenches, "MPS, Xtext, Spoofax — died of ecosystem gravity" (§1.2). Racket's `#lang`,
"Ringua's exact intellectual precedent, and the most complete LOP platform ever built", "stalled at the border of its
own world" (§1.6). The study's "control group", the closed languages that won: "SQL, regex, Make, HTML/CSS, HCL,
GraphQL, Markdown, CUE" (§2).

**Paragraph two, what the model era added.** When a model writes, the field's answer is to check its output, or
to constrain decoding with a grammar or a JSON schema. The record cites the reference implementation of grammar-
constrained sampling by name: "The element encoding and the advance/match algorithm follow llama.cpp's grammar
sampler, which is the reference implementation of GBNF. Nothing is vendored" (`base/neural/SOFTANZA_STRUCTURED_OUTPUT.md`
§7.3, 2026-08-22). General knowledge (mark as such in the chapter): model vendors offer JSON-schema "structured
output" modes, and libraries in the Python ecosystem wrap them; the record names none of these products and holds no
dated study of them. The field's dominant reading of "natural-language programming" is prompting a model; 12.27 says
it: "The field now equates natural-language programming with prompting a model, and the record's claim is the opposite"
(study 12.27, "The reading").

## 5. The decision, and what was refused

From the record's rulings, charters and design documents. "Author" marks the author's words; "session" marks a
session's text the author ratified or that stands unratified.

1. **The kernel act chosen: declare a language, not "use our language".** "Not 'use our language' — declare *yours*:
   the language of your domain, in your jargon" (`vision/01-KERNEL.md`, "The act"; ratified in session by the author,
   2026-08-28). Roadmap line: "the kernel act: DECLARE A LANGUAGE" (`vision/05-ROADMAP.md` line 70, 08-28 20:41).
2. **Refused: an open escape form.** stzu's founding rule: "*the library refuses to define an open escape form*", and
   "No open escape form: no clause, operator or operand denotes host code, calls, eval, or I/O — unexpressible, not
   forbidden" (`stz/declarative/stzu/GRAMMAR.md`, Provenance and "Refused, by design"; session text). The field reason:
   "closedness is the product. An internal DSL that cannot refuse arbitrary host code is an API wearing a language's
   costume" (`ringua/docs/landscape.md` §1.3, 2026-08-06).
3. **Refused: natural language as a declaring surface.** "natural language asks; it never declares" (`06-ALPHABET.md`,
   "N and the ink lines", ratified 2026-09-27); and in stzu: "No natural-language declaration (ringua rule 1 — NL asks,
   never declares)". The governed middle: "N may DRAFT declarations ... a draft enters the world only as a proposal
   judged by the court ... N proposes; the court decides; the human ratifies."
4. **Kept the 2020 promise, changed its mechanism.** "'Executable natural language' as a slogan promised more than a
   court can defend: a natural sentence can be ambiguous and a lie can be grammatical" (`08-NORTH-STAR.md` §1.4,
   ratified 2026-09-27). Study 12.27 rules that the natural layer already satisfies the verdict: "the natural layer is
   a closed language" (session ruling, 2026-10-07 23:44 UTC). See §8: the code qualifies this.
5. **Refused: the machine guessing.** "Only words defined in the semantic dictionary are interpreted. All others
   become literals" (narration `stznatural-vs-ring-naturallib.md` line 122, author's byline). LAW 3: "every ambiguity
   refuses rather than guesses" (`SOFTANZA_INTELLIGENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md` line 193).
6. **Refused: flattening what a grammar cannot carry.** "Nested structures are refused rather than flattened: a
   grammar that accepted what the schema rejects would put the two halves into disagreement" (C9 §6.2, `RC_NESTED`).
   And: "The court does not retire when constrained decoding lands" (C9 §2).
7. **Refused: the agent-governance kit inside the library.** "P5 -- REMOVED by the author (2026-07-12). The governance
   kit (StzPolicy, StzAgentContract, grounded strict) and every LLM-facing relation were ruled OUT of the library and
   deleted the same day they landed" (`NNL_REVIEW.md` §8; §7 "Withdrawn"). Kept: "strict mode, refusal, Why,
   Understood ... as library features, not as an agent-governance product". (Session text recording an author ruling.)
8. **Refused: the M marker zoo, on grammar.** "The M Ruling (author, 2026-07-10): MARKERLESS -- the M zoo is REMOVED",
   "language itself never marks reference this way" (`NNL_REVIEW.md` §4c). And: "an ungrammatical chain that runs is a
   BUG in NNL terms" (§4b, "author's correction").
9. **Refused: a second embedding** (the door, not the facet; chapter 16): "No further host embeddings; the door is the
   answer to 'what about language X'" (study 12.28 quoting the vision).
10. **One grammar, one text, within an altitude.** Haro charter v0.2, ratified by the author 2026-10-01: "Haro has one
   *law* and three *surfaces*: a declared language's text can never contain computing-altitude code"; "v0.1's 'one
   grammar, one text' stands *within* each altitude" (`ringpp/haro/CHARTER.md` lines 109-118). PRINCIPLES §8 rule 3

## 9. Six doors

- **The programmer.** You keep writing code; what you declare is a closed grammar, and the court judges your sentence and
  an agent's by the same rules (LAW 6; C9 Scene 1 judges a declaration before any model is called).
- **The analyst.** The domain's words become a data pack and a vocabulary the system understands or refuses with the
  reason (`Unresolved()`, the strict allow-list: "Operation not permitted in this world").
- **The designer.** Refusal phrasing is declared beside the grammar, in the domain's words (stzu `DEFINE REFUSAL`,
  "refusal phrasing as declared data").
- **The decision-maker.** A model held to a declared grammar was 10/10 valid on first attempt against 2/10, on one small
  model, and a lie still passes: shape is guaranteed, truth is the court's and a person's (C9 §1, §2.1).
- **The educator.** A sentence in Hausa, French or Arabic runs and paraphrases back in its own language; a learner reads
  what the machine understood (`Understood()`; site Offering: "Zara, 9, answers Mission 1 in Hausa", study 12.27).
- **The agent.** You may speak only the declared language; strict mode is your posture, and your draft is a proposal the
  court judges ("N proposes; the court decides; the human ratifies", `06-ALPHABET.md`).
