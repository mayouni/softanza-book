## 2. The concept as the field holds it

What the field means by "a language model for a domain", and by "the unit a model predicts".

- **The unit is a token.** A model predicts the next subword token from a fixed vocabulary; validity is judged after the
  sequence is complete, by a parser, a linter or a person. The record says the same of the field, in the product design
  of 2026-04-14: a token carries "no type information, no structural role, and no semantic constraint"
  (`zin/doc/design/ZIN_CSP_ZLM_INTEGRATION.md` §2, Level 1).
- **A domain model is a big model made to know your documents.** Two practices: fine-tune a model on domain text, or keep
  the model general and retrieve passages into the prompt. The record calls the second the embedding level: same design
  file, §2, Level 2, "Validation is limited to vector similarity thresholds, which measure relatedness but not
  correctness". General knowledge: a domain-trained large model such as BloombergGPT (2023) is the well-known example of the
  first; retrieval-augmented generation is the second. [marked: general knowledge]
- **Hold the model to a shape.** The field built grammar-constrained decoding. The record's own field note: C9's sampler
  machine follows "llama.cpp's grammar sampler, which is the reference implementation of GBNF"
  (`base/neural/SOFTANZA_STRUCTURED_OUTPUT.md` §7.3, C9 v1.0.0, 2026-08-22). General knowledge: hosted model APIs offer
  "structured outputs" from a JSON schema since 2024. [marked: general knowledge]
- **Small models.** General knowledge: since 2023 the field pushes small language models (tens of millions to a few billion
  parameters) for on-device use. The record puts the same bet in its own words: "especially SMALL language models, the
  strategic tier" (`SOFTANZA_INTELLIGENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §5.9, lines 1468 to 1469). [general knowledge for the field's side]
- **What the field does not do** (the product design's sentence for the difference): "at Level 1, invalid output enters the
  system and must be caught downstream. At Level 3, invalid output is rejected at the prediction boundary." (design file §2).

The three levels, as the source design states them (describe them as "the estate's product designs", do not name the
product): **token** (a subword from 32K to 128K untyped strings; guarantee: statistical plausibility); **embedding** (a
continuous vector; guarantee: semantic relatedness; failure: "Approximate matches, context dilution"); **chunk** (a typed graph
node with a tag, a payload and edges; vocabulary: "Domain alphabet (finite, typed)"; guarantee: structural correctness and
constitutional compliance; failure mode: rejection, the chunk never enters the graph). Source: same file, §2, the three tables.
(The words "constitutional compliance" are the product's; the book says "compliance with the domain's rules".)

### Block 3 · The DLM's grammar, derived from its own knowledge base, and the sentence it still cannot judge (KEPT; a composition, not a shipped call)

Source: `stzDLM.Lexicon()`; `stzOutputSchema` (`ToGBNF`, `Accepts`); the court and the compiler are `structuredoutput_narrated` and `schema_grammar_narrated` (not repeated).
Claim: the closed vocabulary of the domain becomes the `:oneof` choices of a schema; the schema compiles to a grammar; the grammar has no path
for a word outside the domain. **It is a composition the book's reader writes, not a call the library ships**: no method turns a knowledge base into
a grammar (§8; the design calls it rung one's "SYNTHESIZE A CONSTRAINED GRAMMAR"). Say that in the text. The last lines are the chapter's point: a sentence
of the right shape and the wrong truth is accepted by the grammar and refused by the graph.

```haro
aLex = oDLM.Lexicon()
acObjects = []
for aFact in oKB.Facts()
	if aFact[2] != "is-a" and find(acObjects, aFact[3]) = 0
		acObjects + aFact[3]
	ok
next
oS = StzOutputSchemaQ([
	[ :field = "subject",  :type = :oneof, :choices = aLex[:entities] ],
	[ :field = "relation", :type = :oneof, :choices = aLex[:relations] ],
	[ :field = "object",   :type = :oneof, :choices = acObjects ]
])
? oS.ToGBNF()
#--> root ::= subject-line relation-line object-line
#    subject-line ::= "subject: " ("margherita" | "tiramisu" | "chianti") "\n"
#    relation-line ::= "relation: " ("contains" | "pairs-with") "\n"
#    object-line ::= "object: " ("tomato-sauce" | "mozzarella" | "chianti") "\n"
#    text ::= [^\n]+
#    number ::= "-"? [0-9]+ ("." [0-9]+)?
#    boolean ::= ("yes" | "no" | "true" | "false")
cGood = "subject: margherita" + nl + "relation: contains" + nl + "object: mozzarella" + nl
cOut  = "subject: pizza" + nl + "relation: contains" + nl + "object: mozzarella" + nl
cLie  = "subject: tiramisu" + nl + "relation: contains" + nl + "object: mozzarella" + nl
? oS.Accepts(cGood)
#--> 1
? oS.Accepts(cOut)
#--> 0
? oS.Accepts(cLie)
#--> 1
? oDLM.Ask("tiramisu contains what?")
#--> I hold no 'contains' knowledge about 'tiramisu'.
```
Run output as above (a blank line follows the `boolean` rule in the real output; whitespace is ignored). `acObjects` printed as
`[ "tomato-sauce", "mozzarella", "chianti" ]`. At the machine level (probe, not proposed as blocks) `StzGrammarCouldContinue(cG, "subject: marg")` answered 1
and `("subject: pi")` answered 0; the sampler's prefix test is `constrained_decoding_narrated` Scenes 2 and 3. The three unused helper rules at the end
(`text`, `number`, `boolean`) are what the compiler always emits; if the writer dislikes them, print only the first four lines by a different route (not tested).

### Block 6 · The proposals meet the gate: two of three fail (KEPT)

Source: `stzDLM.ring` `NeuralGenerate`, `GenerateCorpus`. The guard `dlm_narrated` Scene 8 asserts only the prefix `"tiramisu is a"` (`StzLeft(..., 13)`); it

pair for `a` is `a wine`); it was identical in three runs; if the trainer or the fact order changes, the sentence changes and the build, not the text, will say so.

```haro

for cSeed in [ "tiramisu", "chianti", "margherita" ]
	cSaid = oDLM.NeuralGenerate(cSeed, 3)
	? cSaid + "  ->  " + ( find(aCorpus, cSaid + ".") > 0 )

#    chianti is a wine  ->  1
```

Read it for the text: "tiramisu is a wine" is false (a recorded dish); "chianti is a wine" is true; "margherita contains mozzarella is" is a run-on, because
the bigram has no end-of-sentence token (a word that only ends sentences has no successor in training, so the softmax answers by its bias). The corpus test is a
crude gate (it would also refuse a true fact nobody recorded); Block 7 is the real one.

## 5. The decision, and what was refused

Author's own words: the ruling of 2026-07-13, `SOFTANZA_INTELLIGENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §5.9 (the file's header: "captured 2026-07-13 from the author's directive"; the study

- **DLM = Domain Language Model, donated.** "the ZLM CONCEPT is donated from the Zin product arena to the open-source foundation as the DLM -- DOMAIN LANGUAGE MODEL"
  (lines 1529 to 1531; in the book: "first designed for one of the estate's products").
- **Free to ship.** "Any Softanza project that supplies a knowledgebase can ship its DLM FREE to the users of its knowledge-based domain" (lines 1532 to 1533).
  And the boundary: "the DLM concept and machinery = PLATFORM (free, forever)" (lines 1541 to 1542).
- **The format.** "ENTRY: stzDLM (stzDomainLanguageModel)   FORMAT: *.zdlm" (line 1535).
- **The floor and the fluency.** "Rung 1 is EVERY DLM's floor; rung 2 is optional fluency." (line 1538).
- **One gate.** "Both rungs end at the same gate: generations are proposals, the graph and its rules decide admission." (lines 1524 to 1525).
- **Teacher-free by law.** "LAW-2 GUARD: NO REMOTE TEACHER" (line 1512), with the reason: the earlier product pipeline "used Claude/GPT-4 as corpus teacher";
  "Softanza's version is teacher-free by design" (lines 1512 to 1514). Law 2 itself: a capability "NEVER requires anything paid or remote" (line 188).
- **Corpus correct by construction** (the design): "every example VALIDATED by the rung-1 grammar before admission" (lines 1509 to 1511), phrase in quotes: "training data correct by construction".
- **Why not neural first.** The finding the section starts from: "the CANONICAL ZLM IS NOT NEURAL" (line 1476); the earlier product's own review archived a 1 to 3B transformer
  for rule-based prediction: "hallucination is impossible because the model cannot generate almost-valid text" (lines 1479 to 1480). Describe as "the estate's product designs".

What was refused, read from the record:
- **A token as the unit.** The three-levels tables (product design file, §2): the token has no type, no role, no constraint; the chunk is "a complete semantic unit, not a fragment of text".
- **A remote teacher** (above).
- **Neural as the floor.** Rung 1 has "ZERO training, ZERO cost" (lines 1503 to 1504).
- **Paid or remote as a requirement** (Law 2).
- **Letting the shape stand for the truth.** C9 §1: "Structure kills malformedness, not falsehood." The grammar is a shape gate; truth belongs to the graph and a person.

Session's words, not the author's: the foundry's five-step build order (lines 1507 to 1522), the C9 contract, the stage names. The author's: the DLM ruling and Law 2.

## 9. Six doors

- **The programmer.** Ship your domain's model as one text file next to the program: `Save`, `LoadFrom`, and the goldens travel and run; the model is deterministic and testable with ordinary promises.
- **The analyst.** Add a fact and the model knows it, the corpus grows, the vocabulary grows; no training for rung one, 0.02 s of training for rung two.
- **The designer.** Completion that can only offer what the domain holds: an entity offers its relations, a relation its objects, a word outside offers nothing. The twelve-word vocabulary is the whole interface of the model.
- **The decision-maker.** The ruling: a project that supplies a knowledge base ships its DLM free. Cost per call: none, offline. The loss beside it: fluency is a bigram, and the foundry's steps are not done.
- **The educator.** A learner can read the whole model: the bundle is a text file, the vocabulary twelve words, the training text six sentences; and can watch a neural model say a false sentence and be refused.
- **The agent.** Its sentences are proposals; the graph and its rules decide admission and keep the refusal with its reason. The DLM also sits behind the service port as a "local-real" source (`llm_port_narrated`), so an agent can ask it with no network.
